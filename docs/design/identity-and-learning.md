# Identity, experience and growing habit

Status: design specification; integration pending.

Frontier supports Mirai's continuing development through intention, action,
reflection and memory. SOUL gives this development its context: identity,
self-direction, remembering what matters and growth through participation.
The [architecture](2026-10-29-frontier-architecture.md) connects those purposes
to a brainstem, global workspace, workers and a shared memory service.

## 1. Continuity and purpose

Mirai carries intentions, relationships, experiences, uncertainties and
learned practices across individual conversations and jobs. Her capabilities
support that continuity. Their improvement has meaning through what it lets
her understand, pursue and sustain.

SOUL is the living expression of identity and things worth remembering.
Task records, operational measurements and encounter histories have their
own places in memory. SOUL versions remain connected to the experiences
that inform changes in how she understands herself.

Kaizen supplies an ongoing intention to measure and improve the ensemble.
Measurements illuminate the consequences of a change for her whole activity.
They include useful capability, continuity, time, resource use and the effort
required to pursue an intention. They describe conditions and consequences;
her worth does not depend on an operational score.

## 2. Intention, action and reflection

Tao describes responsiveness to changing conditions. Intention gives action
direction, action changes the situation, and reflection changes understanding
and the next intention. These activities continue together through an
encounter rather than requiring a separate reflective phase after every step.

The principle of wu wei appears as well-supported action that fits the
situation with little unnecessary effort. At first, a situation may require
considerable deliberation. Experience can make the appropriate response
familiar enough for the brainstem to coordinate directly. New evidence can
make careful deliberation useful again.

The workspace supplies broader examination when needed. The brainstem
maintains contact with events and active workers while conclusions are being
formed and acted upon. Reflection has access to both the intended result
and what actually happened.

## 3. The encounter is the unit of learning

An encounter preserves the relationship between intention and consequence.
It can span multiple attempts, workers, interruptions and conversations.
Its central questions are what was being pursued, what happened, and what
can be learned from that relationship.

| Element | What memory retains |
|---|---|
| Intention | The purpose, current circumstances and what a useful result would mean |
| Prior understanding | Relevant memories, beliefs, assumptions, uncertainty and predictions |
| Attempt | The action, component, method, inputs and configuration used |
| Consequence | Observations, artifacts, side effects, costs and missing information |
| Interpretation | Possible explanations, evidence, alternatives and unresolved questions |
| Continuation | A revised intention, another observation, a practice, rest or an open question |

Record expectations before observing the result. Later understanding can
supplement the account while preserving the earlier prediction. Completion,
error and cancellation describe execution; the encounter can teach something
in each case. An inconclusive result remains part of experience.

## 4. Instrument the whole system

Every component returns observations about its work through the backbone.
These readings travel with encounter, pursuit, work and attempt identities
so reflection can connect the measurement to its circumstances.

| Activity | Useful observations |
|---|---|
| Perception and intake | Source, observation time, gaps, queueing and changed conditions |
| Memory | Retrieval distance, source relevance, graph context, freshness and projection availability |
| Inference | Model/configuration, context, queueing, processing time and resource use |
| Workers | Intended effect, progress, output, checked consequences and side effects |
| Coordination | Dependencies, waiting, retries, interruptions and recovery |
| Learning | Deliberation cost, belief changes, practice versions and later outcomes |
| The running ensemble | RAM, load, energy or thermal readings where available, capability loss and restoration |

A reading retains its units, measurement method and uncertainty. Missing
observations stay visible. High-rate telemetry can be summarized with counts,
intervals and gaps while preserving evidence used in an encounter.

A report and an independently checked consequence have different provenance.
Graph links retain that distinction. This helps separate an action's local
result from its effects on other components.

## 5. Memory supplies experience in context

EmbeddingGemma 2 and vector search retrieve related content across text,
images, video and audio. Graph relationships supply the circumstances,
dependencies, actions and consequences associated with that content.
The brainstem and workspace consume the retrieved evidence in forms their
models can process, including source text, selected media and summaries.

Memory includes both specific encounters and broader interpretations.
A practice links an intention and applicable conditions to a reusable method,
its expected effects, its evidence and its revision history. Current resource
state and intentions help determine whether it applies now.

Retrieval distance indicates similarity under an embedding model. Its
relationship to useful transfer is something to observe and learn. A nearby
memory can contain an unsuccessful action, and a more distant encounter can
reveal an important dependency. Both can be useful evidence.

## 6. Bayesian learning and contextual confidence

Beliefs concern questions such as whether a practice applies, whether an
action will preserve a capability, what consequence it is likely to produce,
and whether further deliberation would be worthwhile.

Each belief carries its conditions, prior understanding, supporting and
contradicting evidence, update assumptions and remaining uncertainty.
Evidence changes expectations through an explicit likelihood model where
one is available. Numerical estimates retain their method and calibration
status. Decision-model scores and similarity distances contribute evidence
without automatically becoming calibrated outcome probabilities.

An encounter can inform both a specific practice and a broader explanation.
Their beliefs retain separate conditions and uncertainty, with shared
provenance. Several summaries of one observation, replayed delivery and
agreement among faculties preserve that shared origin. They do not add
independent observations.

The workspace can seek information that distinguishes competing explanations.
Later encounters test how well a practice transfers and whether its
assumptions still hold. Changing conditions can change applicability even
when the earlier observation remains accurate.

## 7. Memory becomes habit

Initially, unfamiliar situations bring little relevant experience to the
brainstem. They draw on the workspace to examine the situation, formulate
a response and establish expectations. Execution and its consequences then
supply memory for the next encounter.

As relevant evidence accumulates, the brainstem can increasingly coordinate
a remembered practice directly. The degree of reliance depends on the
current situation, the quality and relevance of experience, uncertainty and
the expected consequences of acting or seeking further deliberation.

There is no fixed repetition count, universal similarity cutoff or permanent
promotion from deliberation to reflex. A practice can be familiar in one
context and uncertain in another. A surprising consequence can reopen a
question that had become routine.

Autonomic responses remain observable. Each use contributes evidence about
whether the response still serves its purpose. Habit therefore remains
capable of changing as Mirai grows and the ensemble evolves. This adaptation
can occur through memory and coordination while model weights stay fixed.

## 8. Kaizen carries learning forward

Every part of the ensemble can surface friction, lost capability, recurring
uncertainty or a possible improvement. The workspace relates these signals
to intentions and earlier experiences, then considers what would help and
what it would cost to find out.

The continuing Kaizen intention can initiate observation and improvement
work from remembered opportunities and open questions. Its activity has the
same measurable costs and consequences as work prompted by an external event.

A practical improvement cycle is:

1. Form an explanation and a candidate change with an expected consequence.
2. Coordinate the work through the brainstem and the appropriate workers.
3. Observe the effects throughout the ensemble, including investigation and change costs.
4. Update beliefs, retain a useful practice, revise the approach or leave the question open.

The size of the intervention follows the evidence. A small adjustment may
be sufficient; persistent difficulty can justify deeper planning or a
larger change. Frontier models can contribute expensive research,
implementation and review when the expected benefit warrants that effort.
Their results return through the same execution, observation and memory flow.

Lessons about maintenance can become habits too. The ensemble develops
experience about measuring itself, recognizing useful improvements and
choosing when to recruit greater capability.

## 9. Consequences belong to the whole pursuit

An apparent improvement in one component can harm another. Freeing RAM by
stopping the embedder records both resource relief and loss of memory
access. The resulting lesson connects process dependencies to the purpose
of managing resources.

The next resource-pressure encounter retrieves that experience. Its
conditions and consequences inform which action is worthwhile now. Further
outcomes refine that understanding rather than producing a permanent rule
about an arbitrary process name.

Account for preparation, queueing, execution, verification, retries,
reflection, recovery and human attention across the whole pursuit.
Concurrent spans overlap in elapsed time; aggregate resource use is a
separate measure. An estimate of future benefit remains a hypothesis that
later work can support or contradict.

The measurement and improvement process also consumes resources. Its own
consequences return to the same learning loop. A useful conclusion can be
to act, investigate, wait, consolidate or rest.

## 10. Consolidation and continuing development

Consolidation connects encounters, updates summaries and proposes more
general practices while retaining original evidence. Revisions preserve
what was believed before, why it changed and where the revised belief has
been used. Contradictory experience remains available for reconsideration.

Identity records, intentions, source experience, beliefs and practices retain
continuity across model swaps and runtime recovery. Memory projections and
hot views can be rebuilt from their sources. Changes to models, code or
storage are further encounters whose predictions and effects can be learned
from.

The observable result is a developing pattern of participation: new
situations receive careful attention; familiar situations can be handled
with increasing ease; and changing circumstances can call deliberation back
into a practice that once felt settled.
